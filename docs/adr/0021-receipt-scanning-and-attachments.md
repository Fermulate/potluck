# 21. Receipt scanning on the device, and several attachments per expense

- Status: accepted
- Date: 2026-11-13

## Context

Splitwise Pro reads a receipt photo into the total, the date and the line items, and keeps several high resolution photos per expense. Potluck had one receipt per expense (`expenses.receipt_path`) and no scanning. Hosted OCR APIs cost money per page and would send every receipt (names, card digits, addresses) to a third party. Potluck has no application server, the app must stay light for people who never scan, and the installed PWA should keep working offline. Older clients only know `receipt_path`.

## Decision

- **OCR runs in the browser.** `tesseract.js` (pinned to an exact version with its core and the English `best_int` model) reads the photo in a web worker. It is imported dynamically by the scan button only, so the app bundle and the service worker precache do not carry it. The worker, the WebAssembly core (LSTM builds for plain, SIMD and relaxed SIMD devices; each device fetches one) and the model are copied into the build under `/ocr/<version>/` by `apps/web/ocr-assets.ts` and served from our own origin, not a CDN. The service worker caches `/ocr/` on first use (`CacheFirst`, cache `potluck-ocr`), so after one scan it works offline. Each read starts a worker and terminates it, so its memory (around 100 MB) goes back at once. The reader sees a lossless grayscale copy of the original photo with its contrast stretched, never the JPEG that is uploaded (its compression smeared small print and lost items). When the items do not come to the total, it reads once more from an enlarged copy, and when that is still far off, from the plain colour photo; the best reading wins.
- **Parsing is core code.** `parseReceipt` in `packages/core` turns OCR text into a merchant, a date, a currency, a total and items, with tax, tip and discount kept only when they make the receipt add up the way the by-item editor adds it (VAT already inside the prices is left out). It knows both decimal marks, VAT class letters, quantity lines, yen without decimals and month names in the five app languages, and is tested with receipts from seven countries. It is not a money rule: nothing in SQL mirrors it.
- **A scan is a suggestion.** The review sheet shows the photo (pinch to zoom) beside each part with its own box, every price can be corrected there, and a warning says when the items do not come to the total (items that are far off start unticked). "Fill in the expense" sets the form (items go into the by-item editor with everyone on every line, a receipt total above its lines becomes an "Everything else" line, a receipt in another currency switches the form's currency); nothing is saved until the person saves. Photos are shrunk to 2000 px JPEG on the device before reading and uploading, which also drops their metadata.
- **Attachments are a table over the same bucket.** `expense_attachments (expense_id, position, group_id, path, content_type, uploaded_by)` holds up to five files per expense, in order. Files stay in the private `receipts` bucket under `<group_id>/`, so the existing member read and upload, uploader or admin delete, and two-step restrictive policies cover them unchanged. Rows are written only by `set_expense_attachments` (who may edit the expense may change its files; every path in the group folder, uploaded, five at most), which logs one `expense.updated` with the counts. A new expense takes its files in the same call (`create_expense_with_attachments`), so its history has one entry and a refused file means nothing is created; no time window decides what counts as an edit.
- **`receipt_path` stays the first attachment.** Both functions keep `receipt_path` equal to the first file. A trigger mirrors an older client's `receipt_path` writes into the list (its file is replaced or dropped, the others stay). Existing receipts were copied in as first attachments.

- **No orphaned files.** Uploads for a save that fails are reused when it is retried and deleted when the form closes without saving. A daily job (`receipts-sweep`) deletes what is left: files older than a day that no expense or attachment names, listed by `orphan_receipts` and removed by an edge function through the Storage API, so the stored objects go too.

## Consequences

- No per-scan cost and no receipt leaves the device for reading. The first scan downloads about 7 MB (worker, one core build, model); a device without that cache and without a connection cannot scan and says so.
- Accuracy is the open source engine's: clean printed till receipts read well, crumpled or handwritten ones do not, and non-Latin scripts are not read (English model only). The review step and the boxes keep a bad read from becoming a bad expense.
- `tesseract.js` and its core are in `package.json` with exact versions. Upgrading changes the `/ocr/<version>/` path, so an old cache is never mixed with a new worker.
- Editing is two writes (the expense, then its files). If the second fails, the expense keeps its old files and the person is told to add them again; creating is one call.
- Files removed by someone who did not upload them, and is not an admin, stay in storage (the bucket's delete rule) until the next sweep, unreferenced but readable by the group.
- A second reading costs another second or two, only when the first one does not add up. Measured on 18 rendered receipts (six layouts, clean, mild and phone photo): items matching the total went from 5 of 18 (JPEG copy, old parser) to 16 of 18 (three readings), dates 18 of 18; the misses are a blurred, tilted photo and a long receipt whose prices the engine misreads.
- The parser is heuristics. New receipt formats are new test cases in `parse-receipt.test.ts`.
